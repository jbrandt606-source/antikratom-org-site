Colorado Named Five Kratom Products. Ordered Them Removed.

The official bulletin identifies alcohol concentrations from 0.76% to 7.17% ABV. Review the findings, the lab reports and the state-by-state contacts.

Kansas investigators previously identified high-alcohol kratom shots. Together with the linked laboratory reports, these findings warrant careful review by alcohol-control agencies in other states.

Colorado Identified Five Products, Not Just One

Colorado's October 2, 2026 Bulletin 26-03 identifies five tested kratom products with ethanol concentrations ranging from 0.76% to 7.17% alcohol by volume. Testing was performed by a certified and accredited laboratory, according to the Division.

Source: Colorado Department of Revenue, Liquor and Tobacco Enforcement Division, Bulletin 26-03, page 1. Results are for products identified in the bulletin and should not be generalized to every batch.

Colorado did more than issue a warning. It ordered the affected products removed from retail shelves and announced inspections.

What Colorado actually determined

Under C.R.S. § 44-3-103(2), products containing at least 0.5% ABV meet Colorado's alcohol-beverage definition. The Division stated that none of the kratom-containing alcohol beverages had been registered with the state, so the five listed products could not lawfully be sold in Colorado. The bulletin also explains potential violations by retailers with or without liquor licenses.

The Division directed retailers to immediately remove affected products and stop sales, with investigators conducting follow-up inspections. This is a Colorado-specific legal determination, not an automatic finding that every product violates alcohol laws in every state.

Kansas Had Already Found a More Extreme Example

A March 2026 KCTV investigation reported that Kansas Alcoholic Beverage Control identified certain kratom shots containing approximately 15.7% to 16.15% ethanol. The state treated the tested products as alcoholic beverages under Kansas law and took enforcement action.

These Kansas figures should not be generalized to other brands or batches. They demonstrate why actual alcohol testing, rather than package descriptions alone, matters.

What the Laboratory Reports Show — and What They Do Not

The existing compilation includes certificates of analysis reporting ethanol in multiple kratom extract samples, including samples flagged against a 5,000 ppm residual-solvent specification. The compilation is available for regulators to examine alongside original methods, batches, units and reporting limits.

Residual solvent specifications and alcohol beverage laws are different regulatory tests. A laboratory “FAIL” against a USP <467>-based specification does not automatically mean the finished product violates that USP standard, or qualifies as an alcoholic beverage. Finished-product classification depends on the applicable standard and jurisdiction.

What Alcohol Boards Can Determine

1. Whether the measured ethanol content triggers the state's alcoholic-beverage definition.

2. Whether alcohol-related licensing, product registration, labeling or tax requirements apply.

3. Whether retailers or distributors must remove specific noncompliant products.

4. Whether a state agency can independently sample products and verify alcohol by volume.

5. Which agency has jurisdiction when a botanical extract contains ethanol but is marketed as a supplement or shot.
