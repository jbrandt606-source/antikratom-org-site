Ready-to-send message

Subject: NIH Award 1RM1AT013315-01: Explain the $7.5 Million Kratom Research Investment

To the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health:

I request a specific scientific and fiscal justification for NIH award 1RM1AT013315-01 (RM1AT013315), the five-year $7.5 million kratom research program at Washington State University. What will these experiments add to existing science?

Mitragynine is known to undergo metabolic conversion to 7-hydroxymitragynine, a potent opioid receptor agonist. Why is another mouse study a funding priority, and which unanswered question will it resolve that existing animal and human research cannot?

How will the studies address dependence, withdrawal, drug interactions, and the relevance of purified research preparations to commercial kratom products? What prior studies were evaluated to avoid duplicative work?

Please explain the criteria used to select this project, its measurable milestones, and how NIH will determine whether the public received value for this investment.

Taxpayers deserve a specific scientific and fiscal explanation, not another summary of the grant announcement.

Respectfully,
[Your name]
[City, State]

This message is under the NCCIH form's 250-word limit. Submitting an inquiry does not guarantee a detailed individual response.

What NIH proposes to study

WSU's announcement describes research into botanical kratom in relation to chronic pain and sleep, including preclinical work and a planned human sleep study involving chronic low back pain. The researchers plan to use an authenticated, purified preparation. This is research into unanswered questions—not evidence that kratom is an approved or proven treatment.

Three experiments, three questions

The federal abstract describes a chronic-pain mouse model, a healthy-adult pharmacokinetic study, and a controlled sleep study in people with chronic low back pain. Read the official abstract ↗

Where is the added scientific value?

01

Why fund another mouse model without explaining what it can establish beyond existing animal and human evidence of kratom’s opioid activity?

02

Mitragynine can be metabolized to 7-hydroxymitragynine, a potent opioid receptor agonist. How does the study account for this conversion, and what remains unknown?

03

Why prioritize pain and sleep outcomes without clearly explaining how dependence, withdrawal, and misuse risks will be measured?

04

How will findings from standardized research preparations apply to the variable botanical powders, extracts, and other products consumers actually buy?

05

What earlier studies were reviewed to prevent duplication, and what alternative research priorities were considered?

06

What specific decision will each study enable, and what results would lead NIH to conclude that further funding is unwarranted?

Evidence note: Mitragynine metabolism to 7-hydroxymitragynine is documented, but calling mitragynine solely a prodrug would oversimplify its pharmacology. This campaign questions funding priorities; it does not establish that the award is fraudulent or wasteful. The $7.5 million figure is the announced five-year amount, not money already spent.
