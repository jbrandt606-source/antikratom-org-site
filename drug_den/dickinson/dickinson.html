Dickinson College: $93,706 a Year. And a Wall of Kratom Down the Street?

The brochure sells an elite education. The storefront photographs show another side of Carlisle: crowded smoke-shop shelves, kratom-labeled products, and questions parents deserve to ask before paying the bill.

THE CAMPUS

The college brochure has a price. The surrounding storefronts have a story.

For the 2026–27 academic year, Dickinson College lists $71,100 in tuition and an estimated $93,706 total annual cost of attendance, before financial aid. That is the published sticker price, not what every family pays. The college offers substantial financial aid. But regardless of the net price, parents can reasonably ask what products are being marketed in the community around campus. Source: Dickinson College cost of attendance.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SHOWS

Why the wall of kratom?

The Z Smoke & Vape photographs visibly show kratom-labeled merchandise, and the Puff & Bean photographs show stacks of bagged kratom. Other displays contain mixed merchandise whose ingredients cannot all be established from the photographs.

Retail availability also does not establish that Dickinson students purchase or use these products. The college is not responsible for every independent business operating in Carlisle. This feature asks a narrower question: what information should parents have about products available in the community where their children may live and study?

Photo captions describe visible content only. Business information, inventory, hours and routes can change. Google Maps links are searches rather than verified business-profile identifiers. THE QUESTION FOR PARENTS

Who would want their son or daughter to attend this school?

At $93,706 a year before financial aid, parents should know more than what the admissions brochure chooses to photograph. Look at the campus. Then look at the storefronts. Ask why kratom needs so much shelf space, what is being sold, and what safeguards exist.

Dickinson does not own these independent businesses, and their existence is not evidence that Dickinson students use kratom. But parents have every right to ask the question.
