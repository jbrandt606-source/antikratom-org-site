The NDI That Did Not Establish Safety

What FDA actually told TNT Manufacturing about its proposed 75% mitragynine extract.
In 2024, TNT Manufacturing d/b/a Mittwellness submitted a New Dietary Ingredient Notification (NDIN) for a Mitragyna speciosa leaf extract standardized to 75% mitragynine.
Read the Documents Industry Hub

What an NDIN Is—and Isn't

An NDIN is not an FDA approval application. It is a premarket notification in which the notifier provides the basis for concluding that the ingredient will reasonably be expected to be safe. FDA reviews whether the submitted information provides an adequate basis for that conclusion.

What FDA Found

FDA said it was unable to establish the identity of the proposed ingredient because of deficiencies involving the starting materials, manufacturing information, and analytical testing.

FDA then said it was unable to establish the safety of the ingredient from the information submitted. The agency found inadequate evidence connecting the materials relied upon as safety evidence to the actual 75% mitragynine NDI.

FDA's Bottom Line

"does not provide an adequate basis" to conclude that the proposed NDI would reasonably be expected to be safe.
— FDA to TNT Manufacturing

FDA further stated that a product containing the NDI "may be adulterated" under federal law and:

"Introduction of such a product into interstate commerce is prohibited."
— FDA to TNT Manufacturing

That's the regulatory record. No claim that FDA "denied approval" is necessary. FDA's own findings speak for themselves.
