State Policy Investigation Series

KCPA FAIL

The Kratom Consumer Protection Act is sold to lawmakers as the middle ground: regulate the market, set chemical limits, require labels and call consumers protected. This series follows what happens next.

What happens after the KCPA passes?

We examine the statutes, enforcement mechanisms, laboratory problems, changing product chemistry and public-health record state by state. The question is not what a KCPA promises on paper. It is whether the law actually controls the market it leaves in commerce.

Utah · Latest Investigation

Four Deaths. Then They Tested the Products.

Utah passed the nation's first KCPA. Seven years later, officials linked four deaths to potent semi-synthetic kratom compounds and found Schedule I substances in products labeled “cat's claw.” A law can ban the molecule. It still has to find it.

Florida · Latest Investigation

Nine Deaths Later, What Exactly Did Florida Fix?

Florida regulated kratom, declared concentrated 7-OH an emergency, revised its chemical threshold and seized thousands of products. Northwest Florida sheriffs are still reporting deaths—and law enforcement is still developing a field test.

The standard is simple.

A consumer-protection law should be judged by what it prevents, what it detects and what it gives regulators and law enforcement the practical ability to stop—not by the title printed across the top of the bill.
