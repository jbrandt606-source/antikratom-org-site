New York Passed a Kratom Law. Half the Shops Did Not Ask for ID.

18 smoke shops. Nine ID checks. One uncomfortable question for Albany.

New York promised protection for young people. Then a 20-year-old reporter walked into 18 Manhattan smoke shops. Only nine asked for ID. That is what the New York Post reported on October 10, 2026. The reporter did not complete any purchases. The investigation raises serious questions about age screening, not proof of nine illegal sales.

What Albany Actually Passed

Under 21: New York prohibited selling or providing kratom to anyone under 21 through S4552A/A2340A. The February 2026 chapter amendment, S8814/A9472, delayed implementation until 180 days after the December 2025 enactment. The restriction was in effect by the October 2026 investigation.

ID checks: The law generally requires acceptable proof of age, with an exception when the purchaser reasonably appears to be at least 25. A failure to request ID is not automatically proof of an unlawful sale.

Enforcement: New York City's Department of Health and Mental Hygiene is designated to enforce the law within NYC. The law allows a civil penalty of up to $500 for covered violations following the applicable process. Inspection and penalty records are what will show whether enforcement works.

Another law: Hochul also signed S8285/A5852A addressing kratom ingredient disclosures and warning labels. That is separate from the under-21 sales restriction.

A press release is not an ID scanner. A bill signing is not an inspection.

The Officials Behind the Promise

Senator Patricia Fahy sponsored S4552A, Assemblymember John T. McDonald III sponsored A2340A, Senator James Tedisco co-sponsored the Senate bill, and Governor Kathy Hochul signed it. The Senate approved S4552A by a 59–0 vote in June 2025.

Behold New York’s Kratom Regulation.

Albany passed a restriction, declared victory and left the real test to the retail counter. Half the shops in the Post's test did not ask for ID.

The legislature built a barrier. The industry got a sidewalk.

What the Post Found

Reporter Sidney Blasco, age 20, visited 18 smoke shops in Hell's Kitchen. Nine asked for identification; two of the remaining nine asked her age without checking ID. No purchases were completed. The report documents uneven age screening, not nine adjudicated violations.

Five Questions for New York City

1. How many kratom retailers has NYC's Department of Health and Mental Hygiene inspected since the law took effect?

2. How many violations were substantiated?

3. How many penalties were imposed, and for what amounts?

4. What happens to repeat violators?

5. How is the separate labeling law being enforced?

The Age Limit Is Not the Finish Line

An under-21 restriction does not settle questions about adult exposure, dependence, product potency or mislabeled ingredients. The Post separately investigated shops offering products reportedly containing prohibited kratom-related compounds. That is a different inquiry, but it underscores why inspections and product testing matter.

New York can celebrate passing a law. Now show the inspections.
