Executive Action: Kratom Substance Abuse Control Act

What the Governor Announced

On July 20, 2026, Governor Kelly Armstrong and Lt. Governor Michelle Wrigley announced executive actions to ban the unregulated drug kratom in North Dakota. The administration cited growing concerns over kratom's safety, lack of medical approval, and the absence of effective regulation.

"Kratom is an unregulated opioid sold in gas stations and vape shops with no oversight. North Dakota families deserve protection from this dangerous substance. We are taking executive action to classify kratom as a Schedule I controlled substance."
— Governor Kelly Armstrong

Key provisions of the executive action:

  • Schedule I classification – Kratom (mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine) will be listed as Schedule I controlled substances under North Dakota law.
  • Immediate retail prohibition – Sales, distribution, and possession of kratom products will be prohibited across the state.
  • Public health coordination – The Department of Health and Human Services will issue public health advisories and coordinate with law enforcement for enforcement.
  • Legislative follow-up – The administration will work with the North Dakota Legislative Assembly to codify the ban in statute.

This is a major victory for public health. But the fight is not over. The executive action must be followed by permanent legislative action. Your voice matters more than ever. Contact your state representatives and senators to ensure the ban is codified into law.

Why a Full Ban Matters

Kratom is not safe. The FDA has warned that kratom has no approved medical use and is not a dietary supplement. Products contain lead, alcohol, and highly concentrated extracts that have caused addiction, poisonings, and deaths.

The KCPA trap: States that adopted Kratom Consumer Protection Act (KCPA) laws — with "regulatory" frameworks — have seen no reduction in poisonings, hospitalizations, or deaths. The only effective solution is a complete Schedule I ban.

North Dakota now has an opportunity to lead. The governor's executive action sets the stage for a permanent ban. Your legislators need to hear from you.
